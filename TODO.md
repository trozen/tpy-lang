## Deferred to THIR/MIR & workload-gated

Items below are blocked on the THIR/MIR migration (the `cpp_shape` /
place-model end-state) or explicitly gated on a real workload / reproducer
/ named prerequisite -- not schedulable in isolation. `## Next` above still
carries some THIR-aligned *cleanups* that are cheap to do opportunistically
alongside related feature work; only the big-rock deferrals live here.

- **`cpp_shape` redesign (THIR-aligned, post-IR-migration).** TPy types currently decide their C++ boundary form (value / borrow / pointer / storage) through three uncoordinated predicates -- `is_value_type()`, `needs_wrapper()`, `uses_pointer_repr()` -- plus 5+ consumer sites that spell `is_value_type() or needs_wrapper()` disjunctions, plus per-site specialcases (`functions.py:557-568` for wrapper unions, the `RefType.to_cpp_return` qualifier delegation, the `_gen_array_literal` ReadonlyType peel, `OptionalType.uses_pointer_repr`'s force-pointer-repr flag). Every new type-shape concept (recursive-alias wrappers, future variant-form unions, MIR places) re-discovers the decomposition and adds another consumer-site disjunction. The /tpy-review medium pass on `generic-type-aliases` flagged this as the underlying altitude issue. Right shape: a single `cpp_shape` property per `TpyType` (VALUE / BORROW / POINTER / STORAGE) that consumers ask once; lowering methods (`to_cpp_param`, `to_cpp_return`, `to_cpp_storage`) read it and emit. Qualifier types (`ReadonlyType`, `OptionalType`, `RefType`, `OwnType`) compute their shape from their inner uniformly. Codegen never spells the disjunction. Touches typesys.py + sema (the auto-Ref insertion sites must refuse VALUE-shape inners) + every codegen consumer. **Deferred to THIR/MIR migration** -- exactly the kind of refactor THIR is designed to host (per CLAUDE.md's perf section: "THIR is explicitly designed as the natural cache boundary"). Surfaced by the iterative bug-fix passes on `generic-type-aliases` (the recurring "consumer site missed wrapper-aware predicate" pattern is what this redesign eliminates).
  - **Attempted standalone 2026-06 (branch `cpp-shape-redesign`), then the classifier was REVERTED -- the premise didn't hold.** A 3-way `ReprKind {VALUE,BORROW,POINTER}` classifier (design via /tpy-add-feature + Codex review) was built and most consumer reroutes were undone in review: the global-storage sites ask a *storage* axis (where `force_pointer_repr` Optional-of-value diverges from the boundary axis), not the boundary form; and the remaining look-alike predicates (`not is_value_type() and not isinstance(x, (OwnType, TypeParamRef))` etc., ~6 sites) turned out to be **distinct context-specific decisions** (return-Own-wrapping, slot-mutability, has-ref-elements, capture-mode, vararg-is-ref), not one drifted boundary-form predicate. With only one trivial consumer left and no real unification to enable, the classifier was a half-used abstraction and was removed. **Lesson: the "~38 disjunctions" repeatedly dissolved on inspection -- mostly storage-axis, multi-axis (repr+ownership), single-predicate, or structural unwraps; there is little genuine boundary-form duplication to unify.** The full contextual `cpp_form(type, context)` end-state (Codex's sketch) remains THIR's job (`lower.py`); no near-term driver justifies building it on the AST first.
  - **Genuine wins kept from that branch (independent of the classifier):** (1) the wrapper-union return special case was dissolved by making `UnionType.to_cpp_return`/`to_cpp_return_const` wrapper-aware (the `recursive-union-ref-return` merge settled this on the Family C convention -- wrapper returns `X&`/`const X&` by reference, and functions.`_return_type_cpp` no longer special-cases wrappers; the standalone `cpp-shape-redesign` branch's by-value variant was overridden); (2) the motivating tuple-local fresh-member dangle (`t = (i, Box(i)); yield t`) is now **diagnosed** via a per-local dataflow fact `owns_fresh_tuple_member_vars` (sema/context + flow_facts UNION merge + init_tracker; set at the tuple-literal assignment, rejected at the yield/return boundary, pointing at the `Own[T]` element escape) -- NOT via the classifier. Only the *fresh*-member face is fixed; the *durable*-member face (tuple local holding a reference-type member is storage-form and silently COPIES it -- CPython divergence, plus return-build-fail and loop garbage) is a distinct pre-existing codegen bug, now one MED entry in BUGS.md, needing `/tpy-fix-bug`. **Effort 2 follow-ups (deferred, non-blocking):** the UNION branch-merge conservatively over-rejects a mixed `if/else` fresh/safe tuple assign (escape is `Own[...]`); no `tuple_local_own_member_return` happy case yet (settle the return-ctor gap first).
- **Param-const verdict: collapse the body-side re-derivation onto the materialized fact.** `decide_param_const`'s per-param const verdict is now materialized on `FunctionInfo.deep_const_borrow_params`/`const_borrow_params` (`populate_const_borrow_params`, post-Phase-2) and read by the call site, resumable factory field, and union signature. But `compute_body_const_sets` (`tpyc/codegen_cpp/functions.py`) still *re-derives* name-keyed `(const_ref, deep)` sets by re-calling `decide_param_const` -- it needs body-local, name-keyed, `reassigned_params`-aware facts the index-keyed FI verdict doesn't carry. Kept in lockstep only by both paths passing the same `use_readonly_params`; will silently drift if they diverge. Also `param_const._is_ptr_variant_union` hand-copies `CodeGenContext.is_ptr_variant_union` (justified -- no Compiler ctx at Phase-2 time -- but a duplicate predicate). THIR-aligned final form: a single per-param ABI descriptor (the `ParamABI` Codex sketched) consumed by the signature emitter, body, call site, and frame field, so there's one code path, not one fact read by N sites plus a parallel body derivation. Fits with the `cpp_shape` redesign above. Surfaced by /tpy-review of `fix-readonly-union-param-const`.
- **`mark_param_mutated`'s `through_field` field-path climb -> MIR place projection.** The `through_field=True` path (`tpyc/sema/context.py`) strips one dot off a storage key via `_storage_root` (`o.items` -> `o`) to attribute a genuine through-reference write back to the owning param. It's a second string-split climb alongside `effective_storage_through_borrows`, exactly the BorrowTracker string-key shape CLAUDE.md says should be expressed as a `Place::Field` projection traversal under the MIR place/loan model. Works today; fold into the place-model migration. Surfaced by /tpy-review (architecture-fit) of `auto-readonly-usage-clone-selection`.
- **Phase 20 endpoint design (Stage 4c) -- captured, not planned.** Phase 20 shipped Stages 1, 2 (ABI + sema rules + invariant tests), 3 (`raise <expr>` desugar with fresh-construction peephole keeping idiomatic `throw X(args)`), 4a-b (`what()` auto-emit + runtime `raise_X(msg)` helpers replacing the `raise<E>` template), and 5 (slicing-site sema rejection + asyncio `TaskState[T]` / `Future[T]` migration to `Box[Throwable] | None`). The headline BUGS.md slicing bug is closed: mechanism fixed, stdlib workaround removed, new occurrences rejected at compile time. **Stage 4c is design-captured-but-not-planned**: it adds no user-facing behavior (the mechanism is already shipping), and the design walls below give it a poor cost/benefit ratio against current needs. The right trigger to revisit is a concrete future feature that *needs* exception classes to be TPy-defined -- e.g. `@dataclass` on user exception classes, a macro that introspects the exception hierarchy, or some other capability the macro can't accommodate. Until that emerges, the `TPY_THROWABLE_VIRTUALS` macro is small (~20 lines), stable, and approximately zero maintenance.

  **What 4c would complete:** move the 17 exception classes (`BaseException`, `Exception`, `ValueError`, ..., `CancelledError`) from `core.hpp` / `async.hpp` to pure TPy code in `lib/tpy/tpy/_builtins/_exceptions.py`. Drop `TPY_THROWABLE_VIRTUALS` (auto-emit covers all Throwable subclasses now). Replace the inline `raise_X(msg)` helpers in `core.hpp` with forward declarations in `throwable.hpp` + definitions in a new `lib/tpy/tpy/_builtins/_raise.py`. Rewrite the ~40 format-using runtime call sites to bake `std::format(...)` inline (the linker-resolved helpers take `std::string_view` only).

  **Design walls (why this is more than a mechanical move):**

  1. **StopIteration tier-coupling.** StopIteration is a value-type `ReturnException` marker used by the iterator protocol (`std::expected<T, StopIteration>`) in 15+ runtime headers that construct `StopIteration{}` by value. Forward decl is insufficient -- needs a complete C++ type at runtime-header parse time. Forces either special-casing StopIteration as `@native` (with the awkward consequence of dropping `Exception` inheritance, breaking the "StopIteration is an Exception" relationship), or introducing a runtime stand-in marker type with a sema/codegen bridge at the user-visible boundary.
  2. **Method `...` body restriction on non-native classes.** Sema rejects `def clone(self) -> Own[Throwable]: ...` on regular classes with "only allowed on @native classes". After dropping `@native` from `_exceptions.py`, the Throwable protocol's clone/__raise__/what methods need a sema-visibility declaration on BaseException for conformance checking, but the C++ implementations come from the codegen auto-emit -- the stub body is intentionally synthetic. A name-based carve-out for `("clone", "__raise__", "what")` works but smells like the wrong layer; the right design is a "compiler-synthesized method" category with explicit AST attestation.
  3. **BaseException auto-emit symmetry.** Today `_needs_throwable_auto_emit` in `tpyc/codegen_cpp/records.py` excludes `builtins.BaseException` (the historical macro handled it). After 4c that exclusion needs to flip (auto-emit fires uniformly including the root). Small change, interacts with item 2.

  **Cost:** ~2-3 days focused work + ~100 snapshot regenerations. The headline downside is that 4c's payoff is purely architectural -- if it never lands, nothing breaks.
- **State-aware destruction for `frame_slot<T>`, drop the runtime alive bool.** `tpy::frame_slot<T>` (`runtime/cpp/include/tpy/frame_slot.hpp`) currently carries a runtime `alive_` bool that the helper's destructor checks before running `~T()`. The bool is redundant: codegen already knows which frame fields are live at each suspension point. Replace with: (a) liveness analysis emitting an alive-set per `__state` value; (b) the generator/coro struct's destructor switches on `__state` and calls `slot.destroy()` on each live slot; (c) `frame_slot::~frame_slot()` becomes a no-op (caller-driven). Saves one byte + one branch per slot and makes "uninitialized until source-level write" a fully compile-time invariant. Care points: exception thrown mid-suspension must destroy the partial frame correctly (catch path needs the throwing state's alive-set); interaction with pointer-form (`T*`) slots and synthetic optional slots stays consistent. Defer until a workload needs the cycles.
- **Pack `__cancel_pending` into `__state`'s high bit on coro frames.** Every async-def coro struct emits parallel `int32_t __state` + `bool __cancel_pending` fields (see e.g. `tests/cases/async/asyncio_cancel_unblocks_future_wait/expected/include/main.hpp`). With alignment this is 4-8 bytes per frame the bool effectively costs. The bit-packing alternative: states never exceed a few dozen so the int32_t has 25+ free high bits; encode cancel as `__state |= CANCEL_BIT` on cancel, mask via `__state & STATE_MASK` on dispatch in the resumable switch. Codegen sites: `tpyc/codegen_cpp/gen_async.py` (field emit + every state transition + every cancel-check). Cost: every dispatch needs the mask, every state write needs `& ~CANCEL_BIT` somewhere. If multi-threaded async ever lands the read/write would need atomic ordering (today both are plain single-threaded writes, ordering is implicit). Single-byte saving per frame is small in absolute terms but cumulative across long-running programs with many tasks. Touches every async-def expected snapshot in the test corpus -- defer until a workload demands the cycles, or until a broader resumable-frame refactor lands.
- **Heap-allocated slots for `Optional[Polymorphic]` locals with rvalue rebinds.** Local declarations of `Optional[Polymorphic] = ValErr(...)` work for the init-only case (codegen types the slot at the rvalue's actual class type so `&slot` upcasts), but the rebind path raises a clean `CodeGenError` because the shared rebind slot is typed at the polymorphic base and would slice. Phase 20 (above) rejects this shape at sema entirely, pointing users at `Box[Throwable]` for owned polymorphic locals. This entry is preserved as the *alternative* direction (transparent `std::unique_ptr<Base>` slot, one heap allocation per rebind, no source-level change required) -- not planned unless Phase 20's explicit-Box ergonomics turn out to be too heavy in practice. Lives in `_gen_pointer_local_init` and `_gen_pointer_local_rebind` in `tpyc/codegen_cpp/statements.py`.
- **Multi-protocol-diamond const-merge in `_get_dynamic_override_info`.** Current behavior is first-wins (most-derived MRO ancestor's protocol decides const-ness for a given method name). In a diamond where two sibling `@dynamic` protocols declare the same method with conflicting `is_readonly`, the first-visited one wins and the override emitted by the concrete record may silently fail to override the sibling's virtual slot (the sibling's pure virtual stays abstract, derived class stays abstract). Latent today -- no stdlib or test exercises this shape. Fix when a real reproducer surfaces; the right resolution is probably to reject the conflicting-const declaration at registration time, not to silently pick one.
- Per-slot const inference for tuple borrow params. Today `decide_param_const` is per-param all-or-nothing: if any tuple slot is mutated, all slots stay non-const. Per-slot precision (`tuple[mut_slot, readonly_slot]` -> `const tuple<T*, const T*>&`) requires extending `mutated_params: frozenset[int]` to a slot-aware map (`frozenset[tuple[int, int]]` of (param_index, slot_index)?) plus matching propagation in `mutation_propagation.py` and tracking through `_analyze_tuple_unpack`'s borrow edges. Codegen already supports per-slot const through `TupleType._element_to_cpp_param(t, const)`. Worth doing once a real user case emerges where the per-tuple coarseness forces awkward signature splits; defer until then.
- Unify `TypeDef.cpp_formatter` and `@native` / `_native_cpp_names`. They're two implementations of "how to render this type in C++ when the Python name isn't the answer": `cpp_formatter` handles builtins (parameterized: `list[T]` -> `std::vector<T>`), `_native_cpp_names` is populated per-compilation in `codegen_cpp/generator.py::generate` with four kinds of entries: (1) `@native` records with `native_name` -- static per-qname; (2) nested types (`Outer.Inner` -> `Outer::Inner`) -- static per-qname; (3) **cross-module canonical qualification** -- `ArrayList` in one module's view resolves to `::tpystd::tplib::array_list::ArrayList`, but the resolved path depends on the compiling module's import graph (facade routing, etc.), so this is **per-compilation, not per-qname**; (4) builtin stub records (`TextIO` -> `tpy::TextFile`). Cases 1, 2, 4 could move to `TypeDef.cpp_formatter`; case 3 cannot because `TypeDef` is keyed by qname and the answer depends on compilation context. A proper unification requires first splitting `_native_cpp_names` into "static per-qname" and "dynamic per-compilation" tables, then migrating only the static part. Naturally pairs with the per-compilation-state-onto-Compiler refactor below, since both case 3 and the Compiler-instance move require the same "which module am I compiling right now" context plumbing. Also blocked on `@cpp_template` composition for parameterized native records. Defer until the Compiler-instance refactor lands.
- Explicit variant-alternative construction at recursive-union value sites. Codegen currently emits `return 42;` / `return 3.14;` / `return "hi";` and relies on the wrapper struct's forwarding constructor + `std::variant`'s overload resolution to land on the right alternative. Sema already classified the source's TpyType (after the int->float coercion fix), but that decision isn't transmitted to C++ -- it gets re-derived from the literal's C++ type. Fragile: a future variant-member addition or reordering can silently shift selection (same class as the int->float regression, one layer down). Cleanup: thread the resolved alternative through codegen and emit explicit construction at every recursive-union value site -- function returns, list/dict element initializers, field assignments. Forms to consider: `return ::tpy::BigInt(42);` (typed temp; works because the wrapper's template ctor accepts BigInt unambiguously), or `return V{std::in_place_type<::tpy::BigInt>, 42};` (most transparent, no overload-resolution dependency). Skip the wrap when the literal is already typed correctly (`3.14` is `double`; `"hi"` is `string_view`/`string`). Scope is wide: every value-into-recursive-union site, not just returns. Defer until a real bug forces it; current behavior happens to be correct for our existing tests after sema's natural-member fix.

## Open feature gaps
- Named-constant (value-pattern) field comparison in class patterns: `case Dog(size=Size.BIG):` is rejected across all subject kinds (record, union, polymorphic) with a guard hint, while the literal form `case Dog(size=4):` works. Only *literal* field comparisons are emitted -- `_record_field_conditions` (`tpyc/codegen_cpp/match.py`) handles `TpyLiteralPattern` and union field guards, not `TpyValuePattern`; sema rejects value patterns in `MatchAnalyzer._resolve_class_pattern_fields` (`tpyc/sema/match.py`). Workaround: a guard (`case Dog() if d.size == Size.BIG:`). Fix: lower the value-pattern's dotted-name expression (enum member / module constant) and emit `__case.size == <value>`, mirroring the literal branch -- one site in `_record_field_conditions` plus dropping the sema rejection; benefits record/union/polymorphic uniformly since they share that helper. Also extend the dedup/exhaustiveness key (`MatchAnalyzer._field_constraint_key`) to fold the value constraint, as the literal branch already does. Surfaced finishing literal field-value matching.
- Property narrowing not supported: `if f.val is not None: f.val + 1` does not narrow because property access is a method call (could return different values). Same as function calls. Workaround: `v = f.val; if v is not None: v + 1`. Could support narrowing for simple field-returning getters in the future (requires getter body analysis).
- Tuple iteration + Iterable conformance bundle. `for x in some_tuple:` fails sema with "Cannot iterate over type tuple[...]", and passing a tuple (literal or bound variable) to an `Iterable[T]` param fails conformance in `sema/protocols.py`. Real CPython compat gap -- `math.prod((1.0, 2.0, 3.0))` works in CPython, errors in TPy. Coupled fix needed: (1) sema `type_conforms_to_protocol` case for TupleType when all element types widen to T; (2) codegen emits a std::array-style view from `std::tuple<T, T, ...>` for iteration (std::tuple has no begin/end); (3) for-loop codegen dispatches to that view for tuple iterables. Doing just (1) would make sema accept code that then fails C++ template instantiation (the class of bug we've been cleaning up). Workaround: bind to `list[T]` first or pass a list literal. Surfaced while making math.prod/fsum/sumprod/dist take Iterable[float]; those are now done but skip tuple inputs.
- No `AnyInt` protocol covering BigInt + all `AnyFixedInt`. `AnyFixedInt` bounds a generic to fixed-width ints only (excludes BigInt). Writing a generic stdlib function like `gcd[T: AnyInt](a: T, b: T) -> T` that accepts BigInt *and* any fixed-width int has no natural bound. Surfaces when trying to write pure-TPy generics over int types (`math.gcd`, `math.isqrt`, anywhere int-algebraic). Workaround: declare as `int`-only (forces BigInt; small-int callers pay widening cost). Fix: add an `AnyInt` protocol in `lib/tpy/tpy/_core/_types.py` that unions BigInt + AnyFixedInt, with the arithmetic/comparison surface needed for common algorithms.
- Generic type params over int types can't use basic arithmetic without a bound. Pure-TPy `def gcd[T](a: T, b: T) -> T` rejects `a = -a` ("Cannot reassign parameter"; workaround: copy to local), `x = -x` on T ("Invalid operand type for unary '-'"), and `abs(x)` on T ("No matching overload for abs(T)"). Each is because T has no known signed-int bound. Resolving via the `AnyInt` protocol above (with `__neg__` / `__abs__` surface) would unblock pure-TPy integer generics. The ban on reassigning generic-typed parameters is probably separate and may warrant its own review -- for some patterns it's a real ergonomic tax.
- No `Numeric` (or `AnyNumber`) protocol covering `AnyInt + float + Float32`. Blocks writing a generic `math.prod[T: Numeric](Iterable[T], *, start: T = 1) -> T` and similar generic numerics. `start: T = 1` works at sema (int literal coerces to T via normal default-value coercion; verified), so the only missing piece is the bound itself plus its declared arithmetic surface (`__mul__`, `__add__` at minimum). Depends on `AnyInt` above. Once both land, `prod` can be a single generic instead of N overloads, avoiding the empty-list W2 ambiguity entirely. Broader payoff: unblocks user-written generic numeric code (vector math, statistics fragments, aggregation primitives) that currently has no bound to use.
- Atomic shared-ownership smart pointer in TPy: `Arc[T]` (atomic refcounts). Non-atomic `Rc[T]` and its `Weak[T]` companion ship in `tplib` for single-threaded use; the multi-threaded analog is a v3+ item (multi-threaded executor, multi-awaiter Future). Open design: refcount-update memory ordering (acquire/release vs seq-cst on each refcount op), `Weak[T]`'s atomic upgrade compare-exchange shape, and interaction with cross-thread async primitives + a `Sync` bound on T.
- `thread_local` storage in TPy. v1.2 step 7 collapsed the C++-side `executor_ops` global entirely; the only remaining shared async state is `_current_executor: Ptr[Executor]` in `lib/tpy/asyncio/_executor.py`, also a plain (single-process) global. The TLS upgrade is gated on multi-threaded async (v3+) actually landing; until then `thread_local` is purely a future-proofing item, not a porting blocker.
- TPy method bodies on `@native` classes. Today the compiler enforces "methods on @native classes must have '...' body" (`tpyc/sema/analyzer.py:1624`). Constructors via `@overload @cpp_template` work, but instance methods are stub-only. v1.2 step 7 deleted both `Waker` and `ExecutorHandle` from C++ (Waker is now pure TPy, ExecutorHandle is gone), so the original async-side motivation is moot. The gap is still real for other `@native` types that might want TPy method bodies (none on the immediate roadmap). Open question for the design: how the TPy body resolves field access on a C++-owned struct, and whether to allow mixing stub methods + body methods on the same class.
- `imported_protocol_qualification` (`tpyc/typesys.py`) consults `ProtocolInfo.module` (the public re-export module), while the record-side analog uses `RecordInfo.defining_module` (the actual private submodule). If a `@dynamic` protocol ever lives in a private submodule re-exported under a different public name (the `tpy._builtins._bytes` -> `tpy` pattern records use), `qualified_cpp_name` would emit the wrong namespace for cross-module refs. Currently safe because no `@dynamic` protocol uses that re-export pattern; add `defining_module` to `ProtocolInfo` if/when one does. Surfaced by /tpy-review of v1.2 step 7.
- `collect_type_args_of_bounded_records` (`tpyc/codegen_cpp/protocols.py:740`) still has an `info.builtin_type_key is None` guard that its sibling `collect_record_types_from_type` dropped in v1.2 step 7. The two helpers now treat in-module `@builtin_type` records inconsistently: if `Waker` (or any other `@builtin_type` record) ever appears as a type arg to a bounded record, the second helper would silently exclude it. Align the two to the same policy. Surfaced by /tpy-review of v1.2 step 7.
- Generic class specializations -- closed by v1.2 step 5 (Poll[T] port). The premise that `Poll[T]` needed a TPy-level specialization mechanism turned out to be wrong: a single `@nocopy class Poll[T]` body with `UninitArrayStorage[T, 1] + bool` storage covers the value-T / void / reference-T / move-only / non-default-constructible cases by composition of existing primitives. - **[feature] Mixed positional + star-unpack in one vararg call (`f(a, *xs, b)`, `f(*a, *b)`).** CPython accepts arbitrary interleaving of positionals and unpacks in a call (PEP 448): `sum_all(1, *mid, 2)` -> `sum_all(1, 10, 20, 2)`; `f(*a, *b)` concatenates. TPy currently requires a `*xs` unpack to be the *sole* entry in the vararg region and rejects the mix at sema with "Cannot mix *unpacking with other arguments" (`_analyze_and_pack_varargs` in `tpyc/sema/calls.py`; regression test `tests/cases/calls/error_star_unpack_mixed/`). This is a divergence from CPython, not a hard limit; the reject is the correct interim.

  **Why it's not a quick patch:** `_gen_vararg_pack` has only two shapes today -- a fixed `std::array<T*, N>`/`std::array<T, N>` for individual args (compile-time N), and a direct `varargs<T>(as_mut_span(xs))` for a lone unpack. A mix has a **runtime** length (the unpack's size is dynamic), so neither shape fits. Supporting it needs:
  1. **Runtime** (`runtime/cpp/include/tpy/varargs.hpp`): a `varargs<T>` constructor over a runtime-sized pointer buffer (it is already dual-mode with an `indirect_` `T* const*`; add a dynamic-size entry, e.g. from `std::span<T* const>` / `const std::vector<T*>&`, and the value-type analogue).
  2. **Codegen**: assemble a runtime buffer -- push leading positionals (`&tmp`), append each unpacked container's elements (loop / `insert(end, span.begin(), span.end())`), push trailing positionals -- then `varargs<T>(buffer)`. Handle value-vs-ref element modes and multiple unpacks.
  3. **Lifetime (the awkward bit):** the buffer must be a **hoisted statement-level local**, NOT a GCC statement-expression (`({...; value;})`) -- the `varargs` holds pointers into the buffer, so if the buffer dies at the end of the statement-expression the view dangles. The literal-positional temps (`1`, `2`) must likewise outlive the call. This needs the codegen to emit prep statements before the call rather than a single initializer expression.

The alloc count matches the pre-change `_box_coro` path (which boxed the same way, just hidden inside the stdlib helper), so the change is allocation-neutral; the optimization opportunity is reducing it. Two avoidable cases: (a) **`wait_for`-shaped inline holders** -- template `_WaitForFuture[T_aw, T]` over the concrete coro type (mirror of the `STATIC_PROTOCOL` `_CoroParamKind` machinery used for generic async methods today) so `_inner: T_aw` stores by value. Public signature stays `Own[Cancellable[T]]`; consumers become template-deduced. Trade-off: code size grows with the number of distinct coro types passed to `wait_for`. Implementation route: add a `STATIC_CANCELLABLE` shape paralleling `STATIC_PROTOCOL` but with a `Cancellable`-concept constraint (`__poll__` + `cancel`), or just make `Cancellable[T]` available in both `@dynamic` and non-`@dynamic` forms picked per consumer; (b) **`asyncio.run(main_coro)` main-task on stack** -- the main coro doesn't need the executor's slot table (which is for *spawned* tasks). Drive it via templated `poll_with_cancel` directly. ~30 lines of executor change, saves one alloc per `run()` call. The slot-table erasure for `create_task` is fundamental (heterogeneous container of `Box[AnyTask]`) and not in scope. Defer until allocation profiling actually surfaces this as a real hot path.

## Investigate
- Polymorphic integer literals (Rust-style). Today `IntLiteralType` is resolved at the assignment site to `default_int_type` (Int32 by default), so `a = 0; f(a)` for `f(x: UInt64)` requires either `a: UInt64 = 0` or a cast. - **[LOW] Async: support recursive inline `await` via an opt-in heap-allocated sub-future.** Self- and mutually-recursive inline `await` (`async def f(n): ... return await f(n-1)`; `ping`/`pong`) are rejected today (`async/error_async_{self,mutual,3cycle}_recursive_await`): an inline await stores the callee coro by value in the awaiter's frame (`std::optional<__coro_callee>`), so a cycle is infinite-size. Supporting recursion means heap-allocating the *cyclic* sub-future edge -- a pointer member (`std::unique_ptr<__coro_callee>` or a `Box[...]`) is fixed-size and works off the forward decl, breaking the cycle; emplace becomes a heap alloc, reads deref through the pointer. Only the back-edge that closes a cycle needs heaping; acyclic awaits stay by-value (zero-alloc preserved). **Allocation must be opt-in / non-silent** (it crosses the `@noalloc` zero-heap invariant; recursion inherently needs heap, like CPython frames). Runtime caveat: the poll chain nests (`poll(outer)`->`poll(sub)`->...), so deep recursion recurses through C++ `poll()` calls -> stack overflow at large depth (bounded recursion fine). Low priority; Task workaround exists. Needs `/tpy-add-feature` (the spelling is a real design decision). Surfaced by user review of the recursive-await reject.

## C++ Codegen Review Findings (2026-03-12, updated 2026-03-18)

### Systematic suboptimalities
- **[HIGH effort]** Double subscript in augmented assignment: `a[i] += v` emits `__setitem__(a, i, add_check(__getitem__(a, i), v))` -- two separate bounds checks + index normalization. Hand-written C++ would use a single indexed access. Fix needs a reference-based codegen path (e.g. `__getitem_ref__` or direct `operator[]`).
- **[HIGH effort]** Inherited fields body-assigned instead of member initializer list: child constructors without `super()` assign inherited `std::string`/container fields in the body (default-construct then assign) instead of via MIL or base-class constructor delegation. Adds an extra default construction per non-trivial inherited field.
- **[MED effort]** Double `__deref__()` call in auto-deref field access: `r.x + r.y` where `r` has user-defined `__deref__()` emits `r.__deref__().x + r.__deref__().y` -- two separate deref calls. Should generate a temporary for the deref result when multiple field/method accesses share the same deref base in one expression.
### Minor type mismatches
- **[LOW]** Generator yield tuple uses `T&` instead of `T`: generic for-generators emit `auto __val = std::tuple<int32_t, T&>{...}` because the loop variable is bound as `auto&&`. The return wraps it in `std::optional<std::tuple<int32_t, val_or_ref_t<T>>>`, so the implicit tuple converting constructor strips the ref and it compiles correctly. Cosmetic -- the intermediate type is technically wrong but has no runtime impact.
- **[LOW]** `val_or_ref_t<T>` / `param_val_or_ref_t<T>` wrappers are redundant when the corresponding type-param is bounded by `ValueType`. Example: `def identity[T: ValueType](x: T) -> T` emits `template<::tpy::ValueType T> ::tpy::val_or_ref_t<T> identity(::tpy::param_val_or_ref_t<T> x)`. With the `ValueType` bound, `is_value_type<T>` is always `true_type` so both traits reduce to `T` -- the signature could collapse to `T identity(T x)`. Same ABI, cleaner generated C++. Fix: when emitting a generic function/method param/return and the corresponding type-param has a `ValueType` bound, skip the trait wrap. Affects the small handful of `[T: ValueType]` cases in the test corpus (snapshot regen on those alone). Surfaced during /tpy-review of the native ValueType spec-emission fix.
- **[LOW]** Generic-tuple slot emission is needlessly verbose: `to_val_or_ptr<::tpy::val_or_ptr_t<T>>(item)` spells the trait twice because `Dest` must be explicit (the source alone can't distinguish a `val_or_ref<U>` instantiation from a plain non-value `U` -- see the helper comment in `type_traits.hpp`), but `Dest` is always `val_or_ptr_t<T>` / `val_or_cptr_t<T>` OF the type param. Add type-param-keyed siblings `to_val_or_ptr_t<T>(x)` / `to_val_or_cptr_t<T>(x)` returning the trait type, halving the call noise. Optionally also emit one per-generator `using` alias for the yield-slot tuple type, which is currently spelled three times per simple-gen lambda (make_generator arg, lambda return, optional wrap). Same ABI, cleaner generated C++; snapshot regen on generic-tuple cases. Surfaced reading `gen_generic` output during the tuple-unification merge review.

### Performance: JSON parser codegen overhead
Benchmarked with CME MBO order book (15MB JSON, 20K messages). Library-level optimizations (skip_value rewrite, redundant _skip_ws elimination) brought 68ms->59ms. Hand-optimizing the generated C++ (removing checked arithmetic, direct pointer indexing) brought it to 23ms (7.6x vs CPython). The gap is:
- **[MED effort]** StrView/str direct indexing: `sv[i]` where `i` is non-negative Int32 guarded by `i < len` should emit `sv.data()[i]` instead of `__getitem__(sv, i)` with `normalize_index`. The pattern `while pos < len: c = data[pos]` is ubiquitous in parsers.
- **[MED effort]** Checked arithmetic elision for bounded loop counters: `pos += 1` where `pos < len` was just checked, `pos` is Int32, and `len` is Int32 -- the increment can't overflow. Extend `value_range.py` to propagate bounds from loop/if guards.

### Missed optimizations
- **[Open, no work planned]** Generic `T=str`/`T=bytes` param ABI doesn't match non-generic. Direct `str` params lower to `std::string_view`; generic-T routes through `param_val_or_ref_t<T>` keyed on the C++ storage type and gives `const std::string&` -- the trait can't distinguish TPy `str` from TPy `String` since they share `std::string` storage. Same conflation for `bytes`/`bytearray`. Cost: generic-T-over-str pays a `std::string` materialization at call sites (SSO covers short literals; long literals heap-allocate once per call). No fix is unambiguously best; every idiomatic option pays a real cost (distinct types via inheritance breaks `vector<auto_string>` <-> `vector<std::string>` interop; codegen monomorphization is heavy compiler internals; descriptor templates compromise readable-C++ output). Recommended fix *if* the perf gap ever justifies the work: codegen monomorphization (per-call function emission, output stays idiomatic, vector interop preserved). See `docs/IR_DESIGN.md` "Open Questions" item 8 for the full landscape. Surfaced on the generic-U-over-str dangling-view bug.
- **[MED effort]** Proven-safe `T=StrView` inference for pass-through generics. Until the U-over-str dangling-view fix landed, `prefer_strview_for_literals` auto-downgraded `T=str` to `T=StrView` when bare-T params received string literals, on the safety theory that string literals have static lifetime. The downgrade was zero-cost for pure pass-through (`def transform[U](x: U) -> U: return x` with `transform("hello")` produced `transform<string_view>("hello")` -- no materialization). Removed during the dangling-view fix because the signature-level safety check (excluding `Fn[..., T]` / `Own[T]` / record fields) couldn't soundly cover the bug class -- a generic body can construct/store/forward T values, and signature scanning misses that. To claw back the perf, the right shape is **body analysis**: only auto-infer `T=StrView` when the generic is *proven* borrow/pass-through safe -- body returns/yields exactly one of the bare-T literal-backed params, no construction/storage/forwarding of new T values. Use `return_borrows_from` facts if they can express it; otherwise it's an explicit body pass. Today the perf is recovered by writing `transform[StrView]("...")` explicitly. Surfaced when test diffs showed `transform<std::string_view>` -> `transform<std::string>` after dropping the downgrade.
- **[LOW]** Gratuitous named-temp hoisting for non-identifier call args. `_gen_call` / `_gen_method_call` hoist every non-bare-identifier argument to a named local, even when no materialization, lifetime extension, or last-use analysis is needed: `heappush(h, 0)` emits `int32_t __tmp_1 = 0; heappush(h, __tmp_1);` instead of `heappush(h, 0);`. Observed across `cases/stdlib/heapq/expected/src/main.cpp` for trivial int literals, BigInt constructor calls, list literals, and the string case above. Pure source-level bloat -- RVO/dead-store-elim optimizes it at runtime, but it noises up generated C++ and adds work to the C++ frontend (already a compile-time bottleneck). Likely a uniformity decision in codegen to simplify evaluation-order / auto-move analysis. Fix: only hoist when the arg needs materialization (list literal -> vector, literal -> type-conversion), a slot for auto-move, or when two args could alias; let trivial literals pass through inline.
- **[MED effort]** Dead branch elision in codegen: when a condition folds to a constant (`if (true)` / `if (false)`), emit only the live branch body instead of the entire if/else structure. Currently the folded constant is emitted and the C++ compiler optimizes it away, but this adds unnecessary work to C++ compilation which is already a bottleneck. Applies to Literal narrowing (`if mode == "rb":` inside a branch where mode is known), union overload specialization (`isinstance` checks), and any future constant-folding path. Pattern exists in `_gen_if_overload_specialized` for union isinstance -- generalize to all if-conditions.
- **[LOW]** String concat chain produces N-1 intermediate allocations: `a + b + c + d` emits left-associative nested `str_concat` calls, each allocating a temporary `std::string`. A codegen optimization detecting a chain of `+` on string-view operands could emit a single `reserve` + N `append` calls.
- **[LOW]** BigInt method return copies: any method (including property getters) returning `int`/BigInt returns by value (`::tpy::BigInt`), copying on every call. Not property-specific -- `is_value_type()=True` makes `_resolve_return_type` use value return. `str` has `StrView` as a zero-copy alternative; BigInt has no equivalent view type. Fix options: (A) add a `BigIntView` / `const BigInt&` return convention for non-mutating methods, (B) extend `_resolve_return_type` to use `const T&` for `is_expensive_copy()` types in const methods.
- **[LOW]** `__param_` copy for reassigned parameters: when a parameter is reassigned in the function body, codegen takes it by `const&` then copies into a mutable local. For BigInt/string params, taking by value instead would let the caller move. Only helps when caller passes an rvalue; for lvalue calls it's worse (forces copy at call site vs zero-cost `const&`). Also changes ABI (not API).
- **[LOW]** Dead frame-field slot for awaitless-loop iter vars in resumable bodies. Sema's `pending_loop_vars` adds **every** for-loop iter var to `func.generator_locals`, regardless of whether the loop spans a yield/await. The frame-layout emitter (`gen_coro_struct` / `gen_generator_struct`) then emits a slot per name (e.g. `::tpy::frame_slot<Item> it;` visible in `tests/cases/async/coro_for_loop_ref_iter/expected/include/main.hpp`). The slot is never written or read -- the body uses a C++-local `const auto& it = *__beg_0;` instead. Cost: ~16-24 bytes wasted per coro frame per dead iter var, plus tiny ctor/dtor work per coro instance. Correctness was addressed on `fix-iter-var-shadow` via `frame_field_shadows` (which suppresses the body-emit `(*it)` peel); this entry is the perf follow-up. Fix shape: sema-side, scan each for-loop body for internal yield/await (the scan logic already exists at codegen-time as `_for_body_contains_yield`); only add the iter var to `pending_loop_vars` -> `generator_locals` when the loop actually spans a suspension. Side effect: removes the for-loop case from `frame_field_shadows` (the with-as / tuple-unpack cases still need it). Risk: verify no other consumer of `pending_loop_vars` relies on the over-broad classification -- in particular Phase F3's tuple-unpack frame-store gate (`_gen_tuple_unpack`, `statements.py`) keys off `frame_field_shadows` to tell a suspending loop var (real frame field -> frame-store the targets) from an awaitless one (shadow local -> keep zero-copy `T&` references); the narrowing must keep that distinction intact (ideally it makes the gate unnecessary by not hoisting awaitless loop vars at all).

## Low Priority
- **Perf: non-trivially-copyable value types (`BigInt`/`int`, `String`) are *copied* into a `*args` backing array instead of borrowed.** The varargs copy-vs-borrow split keys on `is_value_type`: value types use the contiguous `varargs<T, true>` (direct) mode, so call sites copy-construct each arg into a `std::array<T, N>` (`many(a, b)` -> `{BigInt(a), BigInt(b)}`). That's free for trivially-copyable value types (`Int32`, `Char`, `bool`), but `BigInt`/`String` are const-ref-passed everywhere else (`one(const BigInt&)`) and behave-as-copy without a physical copy -- so a `*args: int` element gets a deep copy of the digit storage it would otherwise borrow. Correctness-neutral; matters only for hot paths passing large `BigInt`/`String` varargs. Fix shape: route non-trivially-copyable value types through the indirect/pointer-array (`varargs<T, false>`) borrow mode like reference types, so the split is "trivially-copyable -> copy, else borrow" rather than "value -> copy". Pre-existing (the packing-mode selection predates the `varargs[T]` body-view type). Surfaced reviewing BigInt vararg copying.
- **Test gap: `varargs[T]` -> `Span[T]` rejection at return / local-binding position.** The `varargs`-body-view tests cover the rejection at the call-argument position (`error_varargs_to_span_param` etc.); the return position (`def f(*a: T) -> Span[T]: return a`) and local-binding (`x: Span[T] = args`) use the same coercion path but have no dedicated test. Add a `error_varargs_return_to_span` case. Low value (same coercion already exercised), filed for completeness. Surfaced by /tpy-review (test-coverage) of the varargs body-view type.
- **`ClassInfo.add_method` mutates the passed `TpyFunction`** (`tpyc/macro_api.py:1211`) -- sets `func.is_macro_generated = True` on caller-owned state. Classic Python alias-mutation pattern; no current trigger because all in-tree callers pass freshly-built functions, but a future macro that re-registers an existing method (wrap-and-replace pattern, e.g. trace/logging decorator) would falsely flag user-written code as macro-generated. Fix: accept the flag as an explicit `add_method(..., is_macro_generated: bool = True)` parameter, or copy the function. Surfaced by /code-review of the super-init UX branch.

- Automatic `Iterable[T]` range-for dispatch via a range-adapter. Codegen emits a single range-for over a helper `::tpy::iter_range(items)` that returns `std::ranges::ref_view(items)` when the concrete type satisfies `NativeIterable` (zero-overhead direct begin/end) or `::tpy::__iter__(items)` otherwise (`native_iterator` or user iterator with `next_iter_mixin`-synthesized begin/end). Single loop body in generated C++; dispatch hides inside the helper via `if constexpr`. Strictly cleaner than both the manual `Iterable[T] | NativeIterable[T]` + isinstance pattern (ugly) and an `if constexpr`-around-body approach (body duplicated). Could collapse the current universal default (`for (;;) { r = __itr.__next__(); ... }`) into the same `for (auto x : iter_range(...))` form for uniformity. Gotchas to resolve: (1) `val_or_ref<T>` unwrapping in the loop variable (needs a deref-hook in `next_iter_mixin::iterator` or an `operator T` decay); (2) move-only iterators (`map`/`filter`/`zip` results) — verify both branches preserve no-copy semantics; (3) rvalue iterables need an lvalue binding first (already done via `auto&& __src`). Infrastructure already in place: protocol narrowing + concrete-type NativeIterable detection both plumbed through codegen. Confirm via benchmark that uniformly routing everything through the adapter doesn't regress anything.
- Native records without `__init__` silently get a positional constructor synthesized from field order. This is fragile -- reordering fields in the `.py` stub breaks callers with no error. Should require an explicit `__init__` stub instead (like non-native records).
- TypedDict per-field `NotRequired[]` / `Required[]` (PEP 655) for finer control than `total=False`.
- `f"{x!r}"` doesn't use the repr() sema fallback: `repr(x)` accepts primitives and user records, but `f"{x!r}"` still requires the `Representable` protocol. Should share the same acceptance logic.
- `= default` semantic gap: records with required `__init__` params currently emit `ClassName() = default;` if their C++ fields are all trivially constructible, bypassing the Python-level construction contract. Should emit `= delete` (or nothing) instead -- `std::optional<T>` and containers don't require default-constructibility, so the impact is limited to direct `T t;` / `T arr[N]` patterns which tpyc doesn't generate anyway. Fix: check `init_params` required count in `_fld_type_cpp_default_constructible` (same as `_is_default_constructible`). See `docs/CONSTRUCTOR_DESIGN.md` open question 4.
- Unnecessary pointer locals for loop-scoped tuple unpack vars: `reassigned_vars` marks a name as reassigned when it appears in two for-loops, even though the C++ declarations are in independent loop-body scopes. This causes `T*` pointer indirection instead of `T&` reference binding. The scopes are independent in C++ so re-declaration as `T&` each iteration is safe.
- None-seeded variable assigned in all branches stays `Optional[T]`: when `x = None` is followed by assignment in both the `if` and `else` branches (so every path guarantees a value), `x` is still typed `std::optional<T>` after the if/else block. Post-dominance analysis could demote it to `T` and skip the optional wrapper. Not a correctness issue -- output is identical -- but adds unnecessary runtime cost and less readable C++.

## Known Limitations
- Subscript narrowing: `if items[i] is not None:` does not narrow `items[i]`. Hard to make sound due to index aliasing and container mutation; would need invalidation on any container write.
- Pointer-local slot reuse: reassigned T* pointer-locals allocate a fresh `std::optional<T>` slot per assignment. The initial slot could be reused after reassignment instead of allocating a new one.
- INT type params on functions: parsing works (`def foo[T, N: int]`) but codegen crashes for non-stub functions -- `inferred_type_args` contains `int` values that `type_to_cpp()` can't handle. Need `type_param_kinds` on `FunctionInfo` + codegen fixes. Stubs (`@cpp_template`/`@native`) work fine.
- Union isinstance narrowing in ternary: `x if isinstance(x, str) else ...` where `x: str | int` would need `std::get<T>()` extraction, which requires statement-level codegen (variable declaration for the extracted value).
- Consolidate implicit-stdlib include filtering in `tpyc/codegen_cpp/generator.py`. v1.2 step 5 added two ad-hoc filters to break a cycle between `_types.hpp` and `coro.hpp` introduced when `Poll[T]` started depending on `UninitArrayStorage`: (a) `_emit_alias_using_block` skips record re-exports sourced from implicit-stdlib peers (mirroring the existing protocol filter, with an inline list comprehension instead of the named `protocol_reexports_external` variable); (b) the auto-include loop skips implicit-stdlib peers not in our transitive reach (`own_deps` minus self-parent prefixes, with a bidirectional `_dep_match` prefix walk). Both are load-bearing today, but: the enum re-export call path doesn't apply the implicit-stdlib filter at all (would break if an enum from an implicit-stdlib peer ever needed re-export); the `_dep_match` bidirectional prefix walk is the third distinct module-prefix matcher in the codebase (the others live in `parse/imports.py` and `sema/context.py`); and the self-parent strip was originally forced by the qname-reach collapse that mapped every record's qname back to its longest registered module prefix -- the `builtin-qname-decouple` work fixed that for records (reach now consults `RecordInfo.module` directly), but protocols, enums, and unregistered parser-placeholder types still go through the prefix walk in `_module_from_qname`, so the workaround is still load-bearing for those. Fix: extract a `module_prefix_matches(dep, peer)` helper to `tpyc/module_names.py`, factor the implicit-stdlib filter into one shared pre-call ahead of all three `_emit_alias_using_block` invocations (records / protocols / enums), and finish the reach-analysis cleanup (use registry-derived defining modules for protocols/enums too) so the self-parent workaround becomes unnecessary.
